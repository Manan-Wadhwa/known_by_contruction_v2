### 3d. The one row that explains the day

`04_temperature_g6`, 80 steps: zero-advantage fraction **0.39 at the start,
0.97 at the end**, with entropy falling 0.0142 → **0.0013**.

The exploration *happened*. The RL *deleted* it. Under cell D the return prices
no mine (`mine_cost=False`), so every deviation from the shortest path is
strictly worse; a group holding one deviating episode hands it a **negative**
advantage and the update sharpens the policy back toward a point mass.

**Zero advantage is cell D's equilibrium, not cell D's failure.** A trial that
ran only cell D, and gated cell A behind finding an ALIVE cell-D arm, was asking
a question with no answer. Under **cell A** the mine is priced: a deviation that
walks around one earns a *positive* advantage, so exploration only has to exist
for the reward to keep the useful part of it.

## 4. What this means

1. **The pre-registered MZ01 RL recipe is dead on arrival, and now we know
   exactly why.** Entropy 0.0176 against a target of 0.70 (a ratio of 39.7),
   zero-advantage groups 1.0000. Six sampled episodes per maze are the same
   episode. Dr.GRPO subtracts the group mean and gets exactly zero. `grad_norm`
   never showed it, because the entropy bonus and the move-mass penalty keep it
   positive — which is why `zero_advantage_group_fraction` now exists as its own
   number and its own gate.
2. **This would have silently ruined the science.** Cell A's entire content is
   the move term, so cell A could not have learned mine avoidance; and cell B's
   "policy invariance" result would have been the invariance of a policy nothing
   moved. Both would have been reported as findings.
3. **The thermostat makes it worse, not better.** An unreachable target means a
   positive error at every step, so the coefficient climbs monotonically until
   it randomises the policy (H 0.76, solve 0.71) and triples the step cost. This
   is HH01's finding (its 14B RL collapses on 4 of 6 seeds against the same 0.7
   constant) one level up, where it deletes the move gradient rather than only
   the entropy authority.
4. **Holding the coefficient still fixes the runaway and nothing else.**
   `fixed` at 0.1 kept the step cost flat and `zadv` at 1.00.
5. **The root cause is upstream, in the navigator.** It was trained by imitation
   of *one* BFS shortest path, so at a fork between two equally optimal moves
   the corpus insists on one of them. A near-deterministic policy is exactly
   what that training produces, and it is why every downstream knob is fighting
   the same point mass.
6. **The trial's design was sound; its cell was wrong.** Every arm did what it
   said on the tin, the arithmetic held, and the answer came back negative for
   an interesting reason. Cell A is the run that was never made.

## 6. What this does NOT tell us

- **Cell A is untested.** Everything above is cell D, where the recipe's failure
  mode is also its correct equilibrium. No claim here transfers to a cell whose
  reward prices the mine.
- **32 mazes per detour bin.** The graded-avoidance claim — "detours when cheap,
  through when expensive" — has to be read off bins of 32. That cannot support
  the claim. Go to 1024 mazes (128/bin) before believing any curve.
- **One seed (0), one cell, one warm start.** No seed variance was measured at
  all.
- **The arms are unpaired after step 0.** The maze draw, word orders and sampled
  tokens all come off one generator, so two arms diverge as soon as their
  episodes differ in length. Differences in end-solve under ~0.05 mean nothing.
- **The temperature arm's off-policy bias is measured, not corrected.** −0.548
  nats per decision at 40 steps, −0.385 at 80. The importance weight is
  deliberately not applied (a trajectory ratio over ~14 replies × ~16 tokens has
  a log of tens of nats; untruncated it is worse than nothing, and truncating it
  adds a clip constant nothing in this programme has tested). The bias
  over-weights actions that are rare under T=1 — which is the exploration the
  arm is for. It is a bias, and it is stated as one.
- **T applies to the whole 150k vocabulary, not to the four move columns.** The
  cost of the temperature arm may land on the *remark* rather than on solve
  rate. Read `unparsed_rate` and `remark_share` beside its verdict; the scorer
  does not.
- **trial80's arm 5 is 54/80 steps**, and its verdict is over the 50 steps it
  had. Not a finished arm.
- **`relative` never got to run its mechanism.** Its coefficient was at 0.0175
  by step 40 — a sixth of what `fixed` starts at. A 40-step trial is unkind to
  the two thermostat arms and kind to the two that act from step 0.
- **Leases die at random.** Three of today's shards were lost to it (two
  killed on purpose, one to a 410) and no result on this page came from a run
  that reached its own end. Everything here is a trace, not a completed row.
