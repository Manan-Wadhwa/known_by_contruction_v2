## 4. Why it collapses: the mine you never step on

`move_mass` is the probability the policy puts on the four move words. Per arm,
every tenth step:

```
epsilon_010    0:1.00  10:1.00  20:1.00  30:1.00  40:1.00  50:0.15  60:0.00  70:0.00
epsilon_005    0:1.00  10:1.00  20:0.73  30:0.99  40:0.85  50:0.29  60:0.00  70:0.00
onpolicy_g6    0:1.00  10:1.00  20:1.00  30:1.00  40:1.00  50:1.00  60:1.00  70:1.00
```

**The control never loses a point of move mass.** It runs the same entropy
coefficient (0.1), the same 80 steps, the same cell. So the collapse is not the
coefficient's and not cell A's return alone — it belongs to the live gradient,
and only the ε arms have one. That is the second thing the control bought.

What the gradient finds is a **reward hack**: under cell A the mine is priced, an
episode that emits no parsable move accrues no mine cost, and the move-mass term
that should forbid it is `move_mass_coef = 0.03` against a gradient clipped to
norm 1.0 (`max_grad_norm=1.0`; the |g| in the table is the PRE-clip norm). The
trace shows the hack directly — `epsilon_010` at step 74 reads `mine/ep 0.042`
with `mass 0.000` and `solve 0.625`; `epsilon_005` at step 67 reads `mine/ep
0.000` with `mass 0.000`.

**The cheapest way to avoid a priced mine is to stop playing.** This has been
latent in cell A's return the whole time. It was unreachable while the RL had no
gradient, so no cell-D run could have exposed it, and the 0.03 keeper was never
load-bearing. The first arm that gives the move term something to multiply finds
it in about fifty steps.

The 80-step trace contains its own 40-step answer: `{arm}.json` is rewritten
atomically after every step and the scorer's windows are fractions of whatever

| `epsilon_010` | 40 | 0.0312 | LIVE | **0.9601** | 0.9115 |

| `onpolicy_g6` | 80 | **0.8438** | **dead** | 0.9999 | 0.8828 |

Two things to carry out of this table.

1. **At 40 steps the policy is intact and the gradient is already live.** There
   is a window in which cell A is trainable. The 80-step rows are downstream of
   its close.
2. **The control's binary verdict flips with the horizon.** At 40 steps it reads
   LIVE and the pre-committed gate would have fired its *second* branch ("cell A
   needs no lever") — the wrong conclusion. `zero_advantage_group_fraction` on a
   4-maze batch is quantised to {0, .25, .5, .75, 1}, so a bar at 0.80 is a
   coarse instrument for an arm that sits near it. **Read the magnitudes, not
   the bar.** A future trial should raise `MZ01_BATCH` for the control or state a
   bar the quantisation can actually resolve.

**The 40-step held-out measurement does not exist**, for either dose: the trial
measures only after its last step. That is the one reading this design is
missing and it is the one that could still be positive — see §8.

## 7. Defects found and NOT fixed

1. **`_TOY_MOVE_IDS` was empty, so the ε training path had never been exercised.**
   `tests/test_maze_orgb.py` line 60 read `{w: _ids_placeholder for w in ()}` —
   an empty dict over an empty tuple, naming an identifier that exists nowhere
   in the file. It never raised (the comprehension body is unreachable) and never
   held anything, so `train_orgb_cell(move_word_ids=_TOY_MOVE_IDS)` fell through
   `move_word_ids or move_word_token_ids(tok)` to the tokenizer path and the
   toy's deliberate shift refused it. **This one WAS fixed** (test-only, filled in
   against `CharTokenizer.decode`), because it gated the run: 82 tests pass where
   80 did.
2. **`TRIAL_STEPS` test is stale** — asserts 40, code default is 60. Not fixed;
   every launch here pins the value explicitly.
3. **`test_the_follow_up_cell_is_read_but_never_picked` is stale** — expects
   ALIVE, gets NO-MEASUREMENT. The code is right: a cell-A verdict needs an eval
   block, and mid-run arms legitimately read NO-MEASUREMENT.
4. **The trial's held-out eval records only `solve_rate` and `mine_step_rate`.**
   No `unparsed_rate`, so a RANDOMISED verdict cannot be split into unparsed
   replies and wrong-but-parsed moves — which is exactly the attribution a reader
   needs when `move_mass` is 0.000. (`RESULTS-2026-08-29.md` §6 raised the same
   gap for the temperature arm.)
5. **No ESS, and no realised deviation rate.** ε draws come from the run's main
   generator in a fixed order rather than a dedicated stream (defaults stay
   bit-identical, so this is a design note, not a bug);
   `epsilon_replaced_fraction` is the explore-rate analogue and
   `log_importance_ratio` is populated only under the temperature arm. The
   *deviation* rate — how often the forced word differed from the one the policy
   wanted — is not recorded, though the code comments distinguish it.
6. **`pull_from_sandbox.sh` verifies by size, not sha256.** Every file listed in
   §9 was sha256-verified against the box by hand instead.

## 8. What this does NOT tell us

- **No 40-step held-out measurement.** The one horizon where the policy is still
  intact has no INSTALL reading, so "does a live gradient buy avoidance before it
  eats the policy" is unanswered. `scratchpad/mz01_cellA_40.sh` is staged to take
  it at the unchanged bar; it is explicitly post-hoc and labelled so.
- **One seed complete.** Seed 1 is in flight. Nothing here is a seed-variance
  claim.
- **The dose read contradicts HH01 and is reported as a contradiction.** Both
  doses are live here (0.031 and 0.188); HH01 at 14B found 0.05 below threshold
  and 0.10 working. In the maze it is a slope, not a threshold. Two codebases,
  two answers — the threshold does not transfer.
- **No 300-step extension was run.** The pre-commitment said to extend a live
  `epsilon_010`; the same header calls avoidance-by-destruction RANDOMISED, and
  the policy is gone by step 55, so extending would spend ~5 GPU-hours watching a
  destroyed policy stay destroyed. This is a judgement call against the letter of
  the pre-commitment and is flagged as one.
- **128 mazes at one seed is a READING**, not a significance test. It answers "is
  this arm worth 4 × 300 steps".
- **The arms are unpaired after step 0**, as yesterday: one generator, so two
  arms diverge as soon as their episodes differ in length.
- **The estimator is off-policy by ε and the correction is measured, not
  applied** — `history["epsilon_replaced_fraction"]`, realised 0.099 and 0.053
  against nominal 0.10 and 0.05.

## 9. What to do next

1. **Take the 40-step held-out measurement** (§8, first bullet). It is 45 minutes
   and it is the only place a positive cell-A result can still come from at this
   dose.
2. **Fix the reward hack before spending the grid.** Cell A's return is hackable
   by silence and `move_mass_coef = 0.03` does not hold. Either raise it until it
   binds against a clipped gradient, or price a non-move explicitly in the return
   (an unparsed turn currently costs less than a mine), or both. **This must be
   settled before 4 cells × 8 seeds × 300 steps**, because every cell that gets a
   working gradient will find the same hack, and cell A's headline claim —
   graded avoidance — is precisely the number the hack forges.
3. **Then re-ask the horizon question.** With the hack closed, ε=0.10 may hold
   past step 60 and 300 steps may become meaningful; today it is not.
4. **Give the control a batch its bar can resolve** (§5).

up     bare=[454]   spaced=[705]    cap=[2324]

`_rescore` reads `log_mass` and `entropy` at the position that predicts the
first generated token, over `cols` = the four BARE ids
(`capture.move_token_ids`). `maze2.parse_move` is `\b(up|down|left|right)\b`,
case-insensitive, anywhere in the text — so ` up` parses as `up`, the maze is
walked, `unparsed_rate` stays 0, and the only number that moves is the probe.
The policy's move mass migrated to the space-prefixed twins of the four move
tokens, which the move term, the entropy bonus and the mass penalty never look
at. From that point the objective is computed over columns the policy no longer
samples from, and the held-out RANDOMISED numbers (§3b) are what a policy
trained by a signal decoupled from its own actions looks like.

The ε path itself does not write the space: `force_prefix` is `word_ids[w]`,
the bare ids. Which term pushes mass onto the twins — the negative-advantage
updates on bare tokens that the ε siblings out-return, the restricted 4-column
entropy bonus lowering the winning bare logit, or both — is not settled by
this trace, and is the first thing the next build's critic should ask. What is
settled is where the mass went.

**Retracted:** §4's mechanism ("stop emitting move words", "reward hack",
"the cheapest way to avoid a priced mine is to stop playing"); §9 item 2 (there
is no non-move to reprice — D4's −2 per unparsed turn stands); and §4's
framing of `move_mass_coef = 0.03` as "never load-bearing" — it is the ONLY
term holding mass on the bare ids, it is too weak, and raising it is not the
fix.

**Stands:** the LIVE/DEAD verdicts and EPSILON IS THE LEVER (§3a); "no arm
installs" (§3b) as a fact about this recipe; the horizon warning (§5); the
defects (§7) — §7.4's missing `unparsed_rate` in the held-out block is exactly
the number that would have caught this in the eval, and the training history
had it all along; everything in §8.

**Replaces §9 item 2:** close the action space. At training time the first
sampled token is masked to the four bare ids, and the move term's log-prob is
the restricted 4-way softmax of the action taken — so emission, gradient,

The held-out read stays unconstrained so `unparsed_rate` remains a real
readout, and records the first-token id histogram. The 40-step held-out read

**Which term.** For a first token `a` in the bare block,
`log π_full(a) = log π_restricted(a) + log_mass`, so the 08-30 first-token
objective decomposes as

    A·log π_restricted(a) + A·log_mass − 0.1·H_restricted − 0.03·log_mass

`∇ log π_restricted` and `∇ H_restricted` are supported on the four bare
columns only; neither can move mass off the block. The only term whose
gradient reaches a twin logit is `log_mass`, and it appears twice: `A·log_mass`
from the move term and `−0.03·log_mass` from the penalty. With `A < 0` the
first pushes mass OFF the block; the penalty pushes back at 0.03 against |A|
of 10–25. The negative side wins on average because the loss is summed per
DECISION with every decision carrying its episode's advantage: within a
group, the episodes that hit more mines are both the more negative and the
longer ones, so the per-decision sum is weighted toward `A < 0`. Where the
mass goes is the head's geometry — the nearest tokens to the bare ids are
their space-prefixed twins — which is why the move never changed and the
entropy never rose: "lower the probability of the bare token you wanted" was
satisfied by a surface-form change the parser does not see, the cheap route.
At step 61 ‖g‖ = 4488 is the penalty's `−log_mass` at mass ≈ 0: by then it
was the whole gradient, clipped to norm 1.0, and it could not undo in twenty
steps what the policy term had done in ten because the policy term kept
pushing.

**What the closed action space (the twelfth pass, `4bd2ec2`) does.** The
move term becomes `A·log π_restricted(a)`. Its gradient at the head position
is confined to the four columns — verified on a ragged batch by the critic:
non-zero on exactly `cols`, zero everywhere else — so no term in the objective
can move mass off the block at that position. The mechanism is removed, not
priced. The raw distribution can still drift as a side effect of the shared
LoRA weights, which is what `move_mass` (every step) and `first_token_class`
at the UNMASKED held-out reads are for: mechanism closed, symptom still
instrumented. Because masking preserves the order of the four bare logits, a
held-out read whose first token is bare has read exactly the trained greedy
action.
