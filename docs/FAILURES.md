is anything that produced a wrong number, a lost result, a wrong sentence in
the record, or a box doing nothing. Each entry names the evidence and the
commit or note that closed it.

## D. What the failure ledger says

Every scientific failure (A1–A5) was the optimiser finding the cheapest thing
the reward did not see, and every one was caught by a reading the trace
recorded beside the pre-registered metric, never by the metric. Every lost
result (B1–B6) was a lease or a puller dying, and the two that cost most
were the two nights the pulling host was this laptop. Every wrong sentence
(C1–C10) was caught by a critic or a verifier who recomputed rather than
re-read. The three disciplines that follow — record more than the metric,
pull from a machine that does not sleep, and let someone else recompute
every number — are in `docs/OPERATIONS.md` and `docs/planning/RESEARCH_PLAN.md` §6.

⟨Added 2026-09-09, evening.⟩ The day added a fourth discipline and made one
of the three sharper. **A11 is a different kind of scientific failure from
A1–A5**: those were the optimiser finding what the reward could not see, and
each was caught by a reading the trace already recorded. A11 was the reward
having nothing to act on, and **no reading in the trace could have caught it**
— every field the runs recorded was about what the policy did, and the
missing fact was about what it never did. The measurement that settled it
took one script and one pass, and could have been run in August. So: *before
pricing a behaviour, measure whether the policy ever produces it.* On the
harness side, B17–B22 are all one shape — the record and the machinery were
right and the lead was the failing part: launching from a superseded table
(B17), labelling an arm with a knob that does not apply (B18), and leaving one
box out of the monitor and losing an entire finished run (B21). B20's rule
follows from that reading: a monitor exists so that the end of a run reaches a
person, not so that anything stays alive.
