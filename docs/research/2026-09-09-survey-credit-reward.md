# Survey 3 — credit assignment and reward design for one rare, low-margin decision (2026-09-09)

**Provenance.** The third of three literature surveys commissioned by the lead
on 2026-09-09. Run by a research subagent with web search and arXiv
verification; the brief described our setting (a 14B LoRA policy, one move
word per decision over ~12–16 decisions, return −2/step, −10/mine, +20 goal,
cap 2× shortest; groups of six with mostly identical returns; a +2 detour
margin against a ±20 solve swing; episode-level credit; entropy ~0.05) and
asked for the credit and reward designs the literature would recommend first.

`experiments/v2/MZ01_maze_world/record/DIAGNOSIS-2026-09-09.md` §2.3.

A framing note that runs through all four sections: your environment has a property that none of the math-reasoning credit-assignment papers can exploit — **the state is observable, discrete, repeats across rollouts, and is resettable.** Most of the machinery below was invented to *approximate* what you can compute exactly.

## 1. Credit assignment at the token/step level in LLM RL

**VinePPO: Refining Credit Assignment in RL Training of LLMs** (Kazemnejad, Aghajohari, Portelance, Sordoni, Reddy, Courville, Le Roux; 2024/2025; arXiv:2410.01679, ICML 2025) — Shows PPO value networks "barely outperform a random baseline when comparing alternative steps," and replaces them with unbiased Monte Carlo value estimates obtained by forking K independent continuations from each intermediate state; up to 9x fewer gradient updates. — This is precisely your fix for problem (3): fork K rollouts *from the fork state* (not from the maze start), average their returns, and the advantage of "detour" vs. "straight" becomes a direct comparison of two V-estimates at the same state rather than a trajectory-level return smeared over 13 decisions.

**Group-in-Group Policy Optimization for LLM Agent Training** (Feng, Xue, Liu, An; 2025; arXiv:2505.10978, NeurIPS 2025) — Nests two levels of group-relative advantage: episode-level over full trajectories, plus a step-level "anchor state grouping" that retroactively finds *repeated environment states across trajectories* and computes a relative advantage among the actions taken at that shared state; >12% over GRPO on ALFWorld, >9% on WebShop, critic-free with no extra rollouts. — The single most directly applicable paper here: your 6 rollouts on one maze all pass through the fork cell, so the fork is a perfect anchor state; grouping return-to-go *from the fork* cancels the ±20 solve/no-solve term that is currently drowning your ±2 margin, and it costs you nothing beyond bookkeeping since you already generate the rollouts.

**Reinforcing Multi-Turn Reasoning in LLM Agents via Fine-Grained Reward Structure and Credit Assignment** (Quan Wei, Siliang Zeng, et al.; 2025; arXiv:2505.11821) — Formalises terminal vs. delayed vs. per-turn reward structures as distinct MDP formulations for both GRPO and PPO, and finds dense per-turn rewards give substantially better training stability and faster convergence than sparse terminal rewards. — Confirms your instinct that episode-level credit is the culprit, and warns that "per-decision return-to-go" (which you tried) is their *delayed* structure, not their *per-turn* structure — the win came from an actual per-turn reward, not from re-slicing a terminal one.

**ArCHer: Training Language Model Agents via Hierarchical Multi-Turn RL** (Zhou et al.; 2024; arXiv:2402.19446, ICML 2024) — Runs two RL loops in parallel: an off-policy value-based critic at the *utterance/turn* level and an on-policy token-level actor inside each turn, reporting ~100x sample efficiency over prior methods and weaker conditions for Bellman completeness than token-level off-policy methods. — Your natural action granularity is the move word, not the token; ArCHer says learn Q(state, move) at the move level and let the token policy inherit it, which makes the fork decision a single Bellman-backed comparison instead of a policy-gradient signal that has to survive 13 decisions of noise.

**Segment Policy Optimization: Effective Segment-Level Credit Assignment in RL for Large Language Models** (2025; arXiv:2505.23564, NeurIPS 2025) — Argues for an intermediate granularity between trajectory and token: MC advantage estimation at segment boundaries, critic-free, with a tree-based variant to amortise the MC cost; 6-12 points over PPO/GRPO on GSM8K. — Says explicitly that segment-level credit lets you "reward partial progress even for ultimately unsuccessful responses" — in your terms, an episode that hits the cap can still positively reinforce the detour it took, which is currently impossible.

**Let's Verify Step by Step** (Lightman, Kosaraju, Burda, et al.; 2023; arXiv:2305.20050) — Process supervision (feedback per intermediate step) substantially outperforms outcome supervision on MATH; releases PRM800K, 800k human step-level labels. — The canonical evidence that step-level supervision beats outcome supervision when the outcome is the only signal; also the canonical warning that they needed 800k human labels to get it, which is why the next three entries matter more for you.

**Math-Shepherd: Verify and Reinforce LLMs Step-by-step without Human Annotations** (Wang et al.; 2023/2024; arXiv:2312.08935, ACL 2024) — Auto-labels step quality by MCTS-style completion: a step's label is the empirical probability that rolling out from it reaches the correct answer, no human annotation. — In a 9x9 maze this label is not even estimated — it is computable in closed form by BFS, so you can build a free, exact PRM ("does this move keep me on a shortest mine-free path?") and skip the whole rollout-estimation apparatus.

**Process Reinforcement through Implicit Rewards (PRIME)** (Cui et al.; 2025; arXiv:2502.01456) — Derives dense token-level process rewards online from outcome labels alone, reading tokenwise reward increments from the log-ratio of an implicitly-trained reward model against a reference, avoiding both human step labels and static-PRM overoptimisation. — The relevant claim for you is the framing: PRIME exists because sparse outcome reward over long horizons fails at *credit assignment and sample efficiency simultaneously* — those are your problems (3) and (4), and PRIME's authors treat them as one problem, not two.

## 2. Reward shaping theory and practice

**Policy Invariance Under Reward Transformations: Theory and Application to Reward Shaping** (Ng, Harada, Russell; 1999; ICML 1999 — no arXiv id) — Adding F(s,s') = γΦ(s') − Φ(s) for any potential Φ leaves the optimal policy unchanged, and this potential-based form is *the only* class with that guarantee; the paper explicitly constructs distance-based and subgoal-based potentials that give large reductions in learning time. — This is the licence to move your mine penalty forward in time without changing what is optimal: set Φ(s) = −k·(mine-adjacency or mine-on-remaining-path indicator), and the agent gets a shaping reward *at the fork*, the exact decision that earned it, while the optimal policy provably stays the one your −10 already defines. Note the flip side: because it is policy-invariant, PBRS alone will *not* widen your +2 margin — it fixes problem (3), not problem (2).

**RUDDER: Return Decomposition for Delayed Rewards** (Arjona-Medina, Gillhofer, Widrich, Unterthiner, Brandstetter, Hochreiter; 2018/2019; arXiv:1806.07857, NeurIPS 2019) — Redistributes the episode return onto the state-action pairs that a regression model identifies as causing it, producing return-equivalent MDPs with identical optimal policies where expected future reward is ~zero so Q-estimation reduces to the immediate reward; reported exponentially faster than MC, TD(λ), MCTS *and reward shaping* on delayed-reward tasks. — Their headline experiment is exactly your pathology (one decision early in an episode determines a reward received much later), and their claim that redistribution beats potential-based shaping on such tasks is the strongest argument for doing decomposition rather than shaping if you have to choose one.

**Hindsight Credit Assignment** (Harutyunyan, Dabney, Mesnard, Azar, Piot, Heess, van Hasselt, Wayne, Singh, Precup, Munos; 2019; arXiv:1912.02503, NeurIPS 2019) — Assigns credit to a past action by the *hindsight probability that the action was taken given the observed outcome*, rewriting value functions through this lens and yielding a family of algorithms that handle credit-assignment tasks foresight-based methods fail on. — Directly targets your asymmetry: "avoided a mine" is an outcome whose hindsight distribution is concentrated on one decision (the fork) and near-uniform over the other twelve, so HCA-style weighting would down-weight the twelve automatically instead of you hand-designing where credit goes.

**Counterfactual Multi-Agent Policy Gradients (COMA)** (Foerster, Farquhar, Afouras, Nardelli, Whiteson; 2017/2018; arXiv:1705.08926, AAAI 2018) — Uses a counterfactual baseline that marginalises out a single agent's action while holding the others fixed, isolating that agent's contribution to a shared global reward. — Read "agent" as "decision index": the same baseline over your 13 decisions asks "what would the return have been had *this* move been different, holding the rest of the trajectory fixed" — computable exactly in a maze by re-solving from the counterfactual successor, giving a per-decision advantage that is zero for the twelve irrelevant moves and ±2 at the fork.

**Difference Rewards Policy Gradients (Dr.Reinforce)** (Castellini, Devlin, Oliehoek, Savani; 2020/2021; arXiv:2012.11258, AAMAS 2021) — Differences the *known reward function* directly rather than learning a Q-function to difference, avoiding COMA's critic-estimation error. — Your reward function is fully known and the environment is a simulator you own, so you are in Dr.Reinforce's easy case: compute the difference reward analytically at each decision instead of estimating it — no critic, no extra rollouts, no bias.

**A Survey of Temporal Credit Assignment in Deep Reinforcement Learning** (Pignatelli, Ferret, Geist, Mesnard, van Hasselt, Pietquin, Toni; 2023/2024; arXiv:2312.01072, TMLR 2024) — Proposes a unifying formalism for "credit" and compares the trade-offs across the whole family (return decomposition, hindsight, counterfactuals, value-based). — Use it as the map: it is the one place that puts RUDDER, HCA, COMA and shaping on the same axes, which is what you need to decide between them rather than trying them serially.

## 3. Variance, zero-gradient groups, and the rare-event problem

**DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale** (ByteDance Seed / Tsinghua AIR; 2025; arXiv:2503.14476, NeurIPS 2025) — Its *dynamic sampling* component over-samples and filters out prompts whose rollouts are all-correct or all-wrong, continuing generation for a prompt until the group contains at least one success and one failure, so every prompt in the batch carries a non-zero gradient. — This is the off-the-shelf fix for your problem (1): keep sampling on a maze until the 6-group contains at least one mine-hit and one clean run, and discard mazes that refuse to produce variance under your sampling budget; without this, most of your compute produces literally zero gradient.

**No Prompt Left Behind: Exploiting Zero-Variance Prompts in LLM RL via Entropy-Guided Advantage Shaping (RL-ZVP)** (Adobe Research; 2025; arXiv:2509.21880) — Rather than discarding zero-variance groups as DAPO does, constructs a non-zero advantage from them — direction from correctness, magnitude from per-token entropy — gaining up to 8.61 accuracy points over GRPO. — The complementary option when your maze pool is small enough that filtering throws away too much data: an all-identical group still teaches something if you shape the advantage from entropy rather than from group spread.

**Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective (Dr. GRPO)** (Zichen Liu et al.; 2025; arXiv:2503.20783) — Identifies that GRPO's division by the group reward standard deviation and its per-response length normalisation are biased reweightings that assign different effective weights to different questions and responses; removing both recovers an unbiased estimator. — Directly relevant to your margin problem (2): dividing by std(R) means a maze where the group barely differs gets its tiny ±2 spread *amplified to unit scale* while an easy maze's ±20 spread gets shrunk — this can either rescue or wreck your signal depending on the group, and you should check whether you are std-normalising before concluding the +2 margin is too small.

**TreeRL: LLM Reinforcement Learning with On-Policy Tree Search** (Hou, Hu, et al., THUDM; 2025; arXiv:2506.11902, ACL 2025 — author list beyond the first two not verified) — Replaces independent chain sampling with on-policy tree search that branches *specifically at high-uncertainty intermediate steps* rather than at random, yielding dense on-policy process rewards under the same token budget and with no separate reward model. — The answer to your problem (4): you do not need entropy 0.05 to spontaneously produce a detour if you branch deliberately at the fork — and "branch where uncertainty is highest" is a heuristic you can replace with the exact one, "branch where a mine is on the shortest path."

**First return, then explore (Go-Explore)** (Ecoffet, Huizinga, Lehman, Stanley, Clune; 2020/2021; arXiv:2004.12919, *Nature* 590:580-586) — Diagnoses hard-exploration failure as *detachment* (forgetting how to reach promising states) and *derailment* (failing to reliably get back to a state before exploring from it), and fixes both by archiving states and deterministically returning to one before exploring. — Your entropy-0.05 policy is derailing in exactly this sense: it cannot reliably re-reach the fork *and then* deviate, so archive the fork states, reset the environment to them, and take the exploratory move there — a deterministic, resettable 9x9 grid is the ideal case for this and there is no reason to pay for it with entropy bonuses.

**Harnessing Uncertainty: Entropy-Modulated Policy Gradients for Long-Horizon LLM Agents (EMPG)** (Jiawei Wang et al., ByteDance; 2025; arXiv:2509.09265) — Observes that policy-gradient magnitude is inherently coupled to step-wise entropy, so confident-correct actions get uselessly small updates while uncertain ones get destabilisingly large ones, and re-calibrates the step-level signal accordingly. — This explains a specific mechanism behind your failure: at entropy 0.05 the ∇log π term at the fork is *tiny*, so even a correct +2 advantage produces a negligible update — the fix is to amplify the update at low-entropy decision points, not only to enlarge the advantage.

*(Note on the "how many samples does a +2 margin need" question specifically: I did not find a paper that states a clean bound in the form you asked for. The nearest rigorous treatments are the general vanilla-policy-gradient sample-complexity analyses — e.g. Yuan, Gower, Lazaric, "A general sample complexity analysis of vanilla policy gradient," AISTATS 2022 — which bound sample count in terms of gradient variance rather than in terms of an event's reward margin and rarity. I am flagging this as a gap rather than inventing a citation.)*

## 4. Grid-world / maze / multi-turn RL with LLMs

**RAGEN: Understanding Self-Evolution in LLM Agents via Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning** (Zihan Wang, Kangrui Wang, Qineng Wang, Pingyue Zhang, Linjie Li, et al.; 2025; arXiv:2504.20073) — Introduces StarPO and documents the "Echo Trap": multi-turn RL collapses into deterministic repetitive templates, preceded by *reward-variance cliffs and gradient spikes* that serve as early warning; StarPO-S fixes it with trajectory filtering plus gradient stabilisation; testbeds are Bandit, Sokoban and FrozenLake. — The closest published analogue to your setup and it reports your exact failure signature (reward variance collapsing across a rollout group, entropy collapse in multi-turn grid tasks); their conclusion that "without sufficiently detailed reward signals that account for reasoning, agents adopt superficial strategies" is a prediction that your mine-blind shortest-path prior will not be dislodged by a terminal reward alone.

**AlphaMaze: Enhancing Large Language Models' Spatial Intelligence via GRPO** (2025; arXiv:2502.14669) — Two-stage recipe on tokenised ASCII mazes: SFT on step-by-step movement commands gets 86% navigation accuracy, then GRPO with a hand-crafted reward pushes it to 93%. — The nearest published match to your task setup (ASCII maze, one move per decision, SFT-then-GRPO), and worth reading mainly as a negative control: it uses a *carefully crafted* multi-component reward rather than a single terminal penalty, which is evidence that the pure "let the −10 do the work" design is not what has been made to work in this exact setting.

**The Entropy Mechanism of Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning Language Models (Clip-Cov / KL-Cov)** (Ganqu Cui et al.; 2025; arXiv:2505.22617) — Derives that entropy change is driven by the covariance between action probability and advantage, and controls collapse by clipping or KL-penalising the few tokens with the highest such covariance. — Explains *why* your entropy sat down at 0.05 and gives a targeted intervention: rather than a global entropy bonus (which will make all 13 decisions noisy and hurt your solve rate), suppress the high-covariance tokens that are driving the collapse and leave the fork decision free to stay stochastic.

**SPIRAL: Self-Play on Zero-Sum Games Incentivizes Reasoning via Multi-Agent Multi-Turn RL** (2025; arXiv:2506.24119) — Fully online multi-turn multi-agent RL for LLMs on TicTacToe/Kuhn Poker/Negotiation, with role-conditioned advantage estimation (RAE) introduced specifically to stabilise the advantage estimate across heterogeneous roles. — Mostly relevant as an engineering precedent for stable multi-turn online RL at your scale, plus RAE as a template for conditioning the baseline on a decision's *type* — for you, a baseline conditioned on "is this a fork or a forced move" would be the analogous move.

**verl-agent** (langfengQ/verl-agent, 2025 — software, the official GiGPO codebase) — veRL extension for multi-turn LLM/VLM agent RL with a step-independent rollout mechanism that keeps context length ~constant over 30-50 step episodes, gym-style parallel envs, and GiGPO implemented alongside GRPO/PPO; ships **Sokoban** and ALFWorld among its environments. — If you want GiGPO's anchor-state grouping without reimplementing it, this is a working implementation on a grid-world environment already, and its step-independent rollout format matches your one-move-per-decision structure better than concatenating full history.

## Summary: what this literature says to change first

1. **Group at the fork, not at the episode — GiGPO's anchor-state grouping (arXiv:2505.10978).** Your 6 rollouts all visit the fork cell; comparing return-to-go *from that shared anchor* cancels the ±20 solve/no-solve variance and leaves a clean ±2, which is your problem (2) and (3) solved by the same change, critic-free and at zero extra rollout cost.
2. **Force the counterfactual instead of waiting to sample it — VinePPO (arXiv:2410.01679) / TreeRL (arXiv:2506.11902) / Go-Explore (arXiv:2004.12919).** At entropy 0.05 the detour will never be sampled: reset to the fork state, roll both continuations, and take the difference — in a deterministic resettable 9x9 grid this is cheap and exact, whereas these papers had to approximate it.
3. **Move the mine penalty to the decision that earned it via a potential — Ng, Harada & Russell (ICML 1999), or full return decomposition via RUDDER (arXiv:1806.07857).** Φ(s) = −k·(mine on remaining path) gives an immediate shaping reward at the fork with a *proof* that the optimal policy is unchanged; RUDDER goes further and reports beating shaping on precisely this delayed-reward structure.
4. **Stop burning compute on zero-gradient groups — DAPO's dynamic sampling (arXiv:2503.14476)**, and check whether GRPO's std-normalisation is distorting your ±2 margin (**Dr. GRPO, arXiv:2503.20783**).
5. **Expect the entropy collapse and treat it as a separate bug, not a symptom** — RAGEN (arXiv:2504.20073) reports the same reward-variance-cliff signature in Sokoban/FrozenLake, and Clip-Cov/KL-Cov (arXiv:2505.22617) gives a targeted fix that does not add noise to your other 12 decisions.
