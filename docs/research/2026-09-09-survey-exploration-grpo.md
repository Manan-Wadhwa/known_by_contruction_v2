# Survey 1 — exploration under group-relative policy gradients (2026-09-09)

**Provenance.** One of three literature surveys commissioned by the lead on
2026-09-09 after the fourteenth-pass tally closed (seven on-policy runs, one
AVOIDS read, unreplicated). Run by a research subagent with web search and
arXiv verification; the brief described our setting (a 14B LoRA policy on a
9×9 maze, Dr.GRPO groups of 6, a mine-blind shortest-path navigator at token
entropy ~0.05, 77–88 % zero-advantage groups, a +2 detour margin against a
±20 solve swing, ε replacement degrading solve) and asked for verified
citations with what each predicts for us. Reproduced verbatim below; the
condensed reading is `experiments/v2/MZ01_maze_world/record/DIAGNOSIS-2026-09-09.md`
§2.1. Verification flags at the end are the surveyor's own.

---

Scope note on our setting that shapes every judgment below: the group-relative advantage is computed over **episode returns**, and a mine-blind shortest-path SFT policy at token entropy 0.05 emits the *same* path 6/6 times. So the 77-88% zero-advantage rate is not a tuning artifact — it is the mechanical consequence of a near-deterministic policy on a fixed maze. Two numbers matter downstream: the detour margin (+2) is ~10% of the solve/cap spread (~+20 to -30), and the detour action has near-zero probability under the SFT prior *by construction*.

## 1. Zero-advantage / all-same-reward groups in GRPO

**DAPO: An Open-Source LLM Reinforcement Learning System at Scale** (Qiying Yu et al., 2025, arXiv:2503.14476) — Identifies that groups where all samples get the same reward yield zero advantage and thus zero gradient, and fixes it with *dynamic sampling*: over-sample and filter, resampling a prompt until its group contains mixed rewards, so every batch is 100% gradient-bearing. For us this is the single most directly applicable knob: reject a maze whose 6 returns are identical and redraw (either more rollouts on that maze, or swap in a different maze), which would convert our effective batch of 4 mazes × 6 from ~0.5-0.9 live mazes per step back to 4.

**Learning to Reason at the Frontier of Learnability / LILO** (Thomas Foster, Anya Sims, Johannes Forkel, Mattie Fellows, Jakob Foerster, 2025, arXiv:2502.12272; NeurIPS 2025) — Shows that across PPO/VinePPO runs most questions are either always-solved or never-solved, and proves that prioritising prompts by *learnability* (variance of success probability, maximal at p≈0.5) maximises expected policy improvement. Predicts we should maintain a per-maze running estimate of return-variance (not success rate — our reward is graded) and sample mazes proportional to it, which for us means preferentially training on mazes where the shortest path *does* pass near a mine and a detour is actually reachable.

**Online Difficulty Filtering for Reasoning Oriented Reinforcement Learning** (Sanghwan Bae et al., 2025, arXiv:2504.03380; EACL 2026) — Derives that expected policy improvement is lower-bounded by the variance of task-level success probabilities and shows balanced online filtering gives up to +12% in under half the training steps of standard GRPO. Implies our 160-step budget is being spent mostly on dead mazes, and that filtering could plausibly recover the same learning in far fewer steps — the cheapest experiment available to us.

**Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training: A Critical Perspective / Dr.GRPO** (Zichen Liu et al., 2025, arXiv:2503.20783) — Removes GRPO's response-length normalisation *and* its group-std normalisation, arguing the latter is a "question-level difficulty bias" that overweights low-variance groups. This cuts against us specifically: dividing by std is exactly what would rescue a group whose returns differ by only 2 (detour vs. mine) relative to one differing by 20 (solve vs. cap); by using Dr.GRPO we removed the amplifier that would have made the mine-avoidance signal competitive with the solve/not-solve signal. Reintroducing std-normalisation (or a per-group advantage rescaling) is a one-line ablation worth running.

**It Takes Two: Your GRPO Is Secretly DPO** (2025, arXiv:2510.00977) and **Scaling Behaviors of LLM RL Post-Training** (2025, arXiv:2509.25300) — Conflicting guidance on group size: the former connects GRPO with G=2 to DPO and argues small groups suffice; the latter ablates G ∈ {4,8,16,32} on 7B models and finds larger G monotonically improves sample efficiency per *unique prompt*, with the optimum shifting with compute budget. For us the relevant asymmetry is that with entropy 0.05 the marginal value of the 6th rollout is near zero — raising G without first raising entropy buys nothing, which is why dynamic sampling (redrawing until variance appears) dominates simply increasing G.

**Don't Waste Mistakes: Leveraging Negative RL-Groups via Confidence Reweighting** (2025, arXiv:2510.08696) and **NGRPO: Negative-enhanced Group Relative Policy Optimization** (2025, arXiv:2509.18851) — Both target the all-negative group (every rollout fails, advantage vanishes) and recover signal by reweighting within the group using confidence or by synthesising a contrast against the all-wrong set. Relevant to our failure mode's mirror image: our dead groups are mostly *all-identical-success* (every rollout takes the same shortest path through the same mine), so the same machinery applies with sign flipped — you need an intra-group contrast that is not the raw return.

## 2. Entropy collapse in RLVR and its fixes

**The Entropy Mechanism of Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning Language Models** (Ganqu Cui et al., 2025, arXiv:2505.22617) — Derives that entropy change is driven by the covariance between action log-prob and its advantage, shows this covariance stays positive so entropy falls monotonically, and proposes Clip-Cov and KL-Cov (clip / KL-penalise the highest-covariance tokens); both are merged into verl as `loss_mode: clip_cov | kl_cov`. Important caveat for us: their mechanism explains entropy *falling during RL*, whereas ours arrived at 0.05 from SFT — so these methods will slow a decline we do not have, and we need something that *injects* entropy rather than conserving it.

**Clip-Higher** (component of DAPO, arXiv:2503.14476) — Decouples the lower and upper PPO clip bounds so that low-probability tokens retain headroom to be up-weighted, directly countering the collapse of the tail. This is the highest-leverage entropy knob for us because our detour move is precisely a low-probability token under a mine-blind prior; a symmetric clip caps how fast it can ever be promoted.

**Beyond the 80/20 Rule: High-Entropy Minority Tokens Drive Effective RL for LLM Reasoning** (Shenzhi Wang et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.01939; NeurIPS 2025) — Only ~20% of tokens are high-entropy "forking tokens", and restricting policy-gradient updates to them matches or beats full-gradient updates (+11.04 AIME'25 on Qwen3-32B). Maps almost exactly onto our task: the forking token is the single move word at a decision cell adjacent to a mine, and every other emitted token is filler — restricting the gradient to those cells is both a variance reduction and a credit-assignment fix.

**Reasoning with Exploration: An Entropy Perspective** (Daixuan Cheng et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.14758; AAAI) — Adds a clipped, gradient-detached entropy term directly to the advantage (one line of code in GRPO/PPO), and shows large pass@K gains; high-entropy regions correlate with pivotal tokens and with *rare behaviours under-explored by the base model*. That last clause is our situation verbatim; this is the cheapest entropy intervention to try and it acts on the advantage rather than the loss, so it does not fight the group normaliser.

**Skywork Open Reasoner 1 Technical Report** (Skywork AI, 2025, arXiv:2505.22312) — Investigates entropy collapse empirically, finds that *strictly on-policy* updates (one SGD step per generation batch) substantially mitigate collapse versus off-policy reuse, and that avoiding premature collapse is necessary for final test performance. Suggests checking whether our update ratio is silently off-policy, and that adaptive entropy scheduling (their MAGIC pipeline) beats a fixed coefficient.

**ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries** (Mingjie Liu, Shizhe Diao, Ximing Lu, Jian Hu, Xin Dong, Yejin Choi, Jan Kautz, Yi Dong; NVIDIA, 2025, arXiv:2505.24864; NeurIPS 2025) — Combines KL control with *periodic hard resets of the reference policy* to a recent snapshot plus optimizer reinitialisation, and shows genuinely novel strategies emerge only over long horizons (thousands of steps), with gains largest where the base model is weakest. Two implications: 160 steps is likely an order of magnitude too short to install a new habit, and if we use a KL-to-SFT term it will actively pin us to the mine-blind prior unless reset.

**POLARIS** (Chenxin An et al., 2025 — project report/repo, *not verified as a peer-reviewed arXiv paper; treat as a recipe, not a result*) — Ramps sampling temperature *upward* across training stages, sizing the increment by how much entropy fell in the previous stage, to keep the exploration space from narrowing. Directly actionable for us: a temperature schedule is a principled version of the epsilon-greedy we tried, and unlike epsilon it perturbs the policy's own distribution rather than overwriting moves with uniform-random ones.

**Revisiting Entropy Regularization: Adaptive Coefficient Unlocks Its Potential** (2025, arXiv:2510.10959) and **Rethinking Entropy Regularization in Large Reasoning Models** (2025, arXiv:2509.25133) — Document that a plain entropy bonus is pathologically coefficient-sensitive (too small → collapse persists, too large → entropy explosion and degradation, with the sign of the effect flipping under small setup changes), and that naive entropy loss flattens the whole vocabulary uninformatively. Predicts that if we add an entropy bonus we should target a *level* adaptively rather than fix a coefficient, and expect the naive version to reproduce our epsilon-greedy failure (solve rate collapse) for the same reason.

## 3. "RL only sharpens what the base can sample" — and the off-policy answers

**Does Reinforcement Learning Really Incentivize Reasoning Capacity in LLMs Beyond the Base Model?** (Yang Yue, Zhiqi Chen, Rui Lu, Andrew Zhao, Zhaokai Wang, Shiji Song, Gao Huang, 2025, arXiv:2504.13837; NeurIPS 2025) — RLVR-trained models beat the base at pass@1 but the *base* wins at large k, across six RLVR algorithms — RL narrows the support rather than extending it. The sharp prediction for us: if a mine-avoiding detour is not in the SFT navigator's top-k samples at temperature ~1, no amount of Dr.GRPO on the -10 will conjure it, and our 10-20% mine drift is roughly the ceiling of what re-weighting existing support can deliver.

**Echo Chamber: RL Post-training Amplifies Behaviors Learned in Pretraining** (Rosie Zhao, Alexandru Meterez, Sham Kakade, Cengiz Pehlevan, Samy Jelassi, Eran Malach, 2025, arXiv:2504.07912) — In controlled pretraining-data experiments, RL converges to a dominant output distribution that amplifies patterns already present upstream. Our upstream is BFS shortest paths that walk *through* mines, so the amplified mode is exactly the behaviour we are trying to remove — the SFT stage is the adversary here, not the starting gift.

**Spurious Rewards: Rethinking Training Signals in RLVR** (Rulin Shao et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.10947) — Random, format-only and even *incorrect-label* rewards recover most of the gain of ground-truth rewards on Qwen2.5-Math (e.g. +21.4% from random reward vs +29.1% from true reward), but only for model families whose latent behaviour is already there. A useful and cheap control for us: run one seed with the mine penalty set to 0 (keep -2/step and +20/goal). If mine rate still drifts down 10-20%, our "learning" is not coming from the -10 at all.

**Learning to Reason under Off-Policy Guidance / LUFFY** (Jianhao Yan, Yafu Li, Zican Hu et al., 2025, arXiv:2504.14945) — Mixes off-policy expert traces into the GRPO group during advantage estimation, with regularised-importance-sampling "policy shaping" that up-weights low-probability but crucial actions; critically, it trains weak models *in regimes where on-policy RLVR completely fails*. This is the most direct prescription for our setting: generate mine-aware paths with a BFS that treats mines as cost-10 cells, inject 1-2 of them into each group of 6, and the group instantly has return variance *and* a positive-advantage trace demonstrating the detour.

**Rewarding the Unlikely: Lifting GRPO Beyond Distribution Sharpening** (Andre He, Daniel Fried, Sean Welleck, 2025, arXiv:2506.02355) — Names a *rank bias* in GRPO whereby high-probability correct trajectories are reinforced and rare correct ones are neglected, and fixes it with an "unlikeliness reward" that explicitly up-weights rare-but-correct solutions. Predicts that on the rare occasion one of our 6 rollouts does detour successfully, standard GRPO under-credits it relative to the 5 familiar ones; up-weighting by inverse trajectory likelihood is a ~5-line change.

**QuestA: Expanding Reasoning Capacity in LLMs via Question Augmentation** (Jiazheng Li et al., 2025, arXiv:2507.13266) — Augments each question with a *partial solution* during RL, which both densifies the reward and raises the chance of sampling an informative trajectory; improves pass@1 *and* pass@k precisely on problems where standard RL stalls. Our analogue: start a fraction of rollouts from a prefix that has already been walked to the cell just before the mine, so the model only has to produce the detour decision, not the whole path.

**BREAD: Branched Rollouts from Expert Anchors Bridge SFT & RL for Reasoning** (Xuechen Zhang et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.17211; NeurIPS 2025) — Branches rollouts from intermediate states of an expert trace and *adaptively inserts short expert prefixes when self-generated traces fail*, guaranteeing each update contains at least one successful trace; needs <40% of the ground-truth traces and trains ~3x faster than GRPO. This is a drop-in guarantee against our zero-advantage groups — the "at least one successful trace per group" invariant is exactly what our 77-88% dead-group statistic is missing.

**Blending Supervised and Reinforcement Fine-Tuning with Prefix Sampling / Prefix-RFT** (Zeyu Huang, Tianhao Cheng, Zihan Qiu, Zili Wang, Yinghui Xu, Edoardo Ponti, Ivan Titov, 2025, arXiv:2507.01679); **ReLIFT: Learning What Reinforcement Learning Can't** (Lu Ma, Hao Liang et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.07527; ICLR 2026); **SRFT: single-stage supervised + reinforcement fine-tuning** (2025, arXiv:2506.19767 — *id taken from a citing paper's bibliography, not verified on the abstract page*) — Three variants of the same principle: seed rollouts with an expert prefix and RL the continuation (Prefix-RFT), interleave SFT on the questions RL cannot solve (ReLIFT, using 13% of the demo data of pure SFT), or jointly weight both losses per batch by entropy (SRFT). Together they say the right shape for us is a *mine-aware* SFT stream running alongside RL on the mazes where RL produces no variance, rather than either alone. (One 2026 study, arXiv:2604.23747, argues a properly-tuned SFT-then-RL baseline beats all three in-distribution — worth knowing before over-engineering.)

**Training LLMs for Reasoning through Reverse Curriculum RL / R³** (Zhiheng Xi et al., 2024, arXiv:2402.05808; ICML 2024) — Slides the RL start state backwards from the *end* of a demonstration to its beginning, so outcome-only supervision yields step-level signal and exploration is easy at every stage; +4.1 points average over the RL baseline on Llama2-7B. Ideal fit for a maze: begin episodes one cell before the mine (where the detour decision is trivially discoverable in 6 samples) and anneal the start position back to the maze entrance.

**STaR: Bootstrapping Reasoning With Reasoning** (Eric Zelikman, Yuhuai Wu, Jesse Mu, Noah Goodman, 2022, arXiv:2203.14465), with rejection-sampling fine-tuning as its filtered variant — Generate many samples, keep the ones that verify, SFT on them, repeat; the rationalisation step re-prompts with the answer as a hint when the model fails. For us the "hint" is the mine location, and the loop is: sample at high temperature, keep only mine-free solving paths, SFT on them, then hand the result to RL — an expert-iteration bootstrap that does not depend on any single group having variance.

## 4. Sparse / rare-event exploration from classic deep RL

**First return, then explore / Go-Explore** (Adrien Ecoffet, Joost Huizinga, Joel Lehman, Kenneth Stanley, Jeff Clune; *Nature* 590:580-586, 2021; earlier arXiv:1901.10995 and arXiv:2004.12919) — Names the two failure modes of naive exploration as *detachment* (forgetting how to reach a promising state) and *derailment* (randomising on the way to it instead of reliably returning first), and fixes both by archiving states and deterministically returning before exploring. This is the precise diagnosis of why our epsilon-greedy collapsed: replacing 10-30% of moves uniformly at random derails the agent en route, destroying the +20 far more often than it discovers a -10-avoiding detour. The prescription is to explore *only at the decision point* (deterministic prefix, then perturb one move), which is a much narrower intervention than epsilon-over-all-moves.

**Self-Imitation Learning** (Junhyuk Oh, Yijie Guo, Satinder Singh, Honglak Lee, 2018, arXiv:1806.05635; ICML 2018) — Adds a loss that imitates the agent's own past decisions *only when the realised return exceeded the value estimate*, giving strong gains on hard-exploration Atari; the stated motivation is that in sparse-reward settings it is not enough to occasionally get the reward, you must exploit that rare experience hard enough to make it reproducible. For us: keep a small buffer of the rare rollouts that solved *and* avoided a mine, and replay them with a positive-advantage-only loss — this is the cheapest way to convert a 1-in-many lucky detour into a habit.

**Exploration by Random Network Distillation** (Yuri Burda, Harrison Edwards, Amos Storkey, Oleg Klimov, 2018, arXiv:1810.12894; ICLR 2019), **Unifying Count-Based Exploration and Intrinsic Motivation** (Marc Bellemare, Sriram Srinivasan, Georg Ostrovski, Tom Schaul, David Saxton, Rémi Munos, 2016, arXiv:1606.01868; NeurIPS 2016), **Curiosity-driven Exploration by Self-supervised Prediction / ICM** (Deepak Pathak, Pulkit Agrawal, Alexei Efros, Trevor Darrell, 2017, arXiv:1705.05363; ICML 2017) — The three canonical novelty bonuses: prediction error against a fixed random net, pseudo-counts from a density model, and forward-model error in an inverse-dynamics feature space; all crack Montezuma's Revenge-class sparsity. In a 9x9 grid the pseudo-count reduces to an *exact* visit count over (maze, cell) pairs — we can add a genuine count-based bonus with a dictionary, and it would pay the agent for stepping onto the off-shortest-path cells that a detour requires, which is exactly the region the SFT prior never visits.

**Hindsight Experience Replay** (Marcin Andrychowicz et al., 2017, arXiv:1707.01495; NeurIPS 2017) — Relabels failed trajectories as successful with respect to whichever goal was actually achieved, turning every rollout into signal under sparse binary reward, and acts as an implicit curriculum. The maze-native version for us: a rollout that hit the step cap can be relabelled as a successful episode to the cell where it stopped, which manufactures return variance in groups that currently produce none — though note our reward is graded rather than binary, so this needs a goal-conditioned prompt to be well-posed.

**Policy Invariance Under Reward Transformations: Theory and Application to Reward Shaping** (Andrew Ng, Daishi Harada, Stuart Russell, 1999, ICML) — Proves that a shaping term of the form F(s,s') = γΦ(s') − Φ(s) leaves the optimal policy set unchanged, and that this potential-based family is *exactly* the set of transformations with that guarantee. Licenses us to add a dense mine-proximity potential (e.g. Φ decreasing near mines) to sharpen the local signal without changing what the optimal policy is — a principled alternative to simply raising the mine price from -10 to, say, -30 to widen the +2 margin.

**Reinforcing Multi-Turn Reasoning in LLM Agents via Turn-Level Credit Assignment / Reward Design** (Siliang Zeng et al., 2025, arXiv:2505.11821) — Finds that for both GRPO and PPO, *dense per-turn* rewards consistently beat sparse terminal and delayed rewards in stability and convergence, because trajectory-level returns merge outcome and intermediate signal into one number and wreck advantage estimation. This is the general form of our specific problem: our per-step -2 and per-mine -10 exist but get summed into a single episode return before the group normaliser sees them, so the mine decision is never credited separately — assigning advantage at the decision that stepped on the mine is the structural fix.

## What the literature would predict about our failure

1. **The zero-advantage rate is the binding constraint and it is structural, not a hyperparameter.** DAPO, LILO and online difficulty filtering all say the same thing: 6 samples from a 0.05-entropy policy on a fixed maze are one sample, and until you either resample until the group has return variance or filter mazes by variance, ~80% of every batch is arithmetically guaranteed to contribute nothing — so the gradient "dying" is not a pathology to debug, it is the expected behaviour.

2. **Our entropy problem is inherited, not induced, so the fashionable entropy fixes will underdeliver.** Cui et al., Skywork-OR1 and ProRL all treat entropy as something that *falls during RL*; ours arrived at 0.05 from SFT on deterministic BFS paths. Only the interventions that raise entropy or promote the tail — Clip-Higher, entropy-in-the-advantage (arXiv:2506.14758), POLARIS-style temperature ramps, and gradient restriction to forking tokens — address the level rather than the slope.

3. **A mine-blind prior cannot be sharpened into a mine-avoider.** Yue et al., Echo Chamber and the rank-bias result jointly predict that the detour has near-zero mass in the SFT policy, that GRPO only re-weights existing mass, and that 10-20% mine drift is therefore close to the ceiling — the escape is off-policy guidance (LUFFY, Prefix-RFT, BREAD, R³, QuestA), which is also the only family of methods reported to work *where on-policy RLVR completely fails*.

4. **Epsilon-greedy failed for the reason Go-Explore named twenty-two years after the problem appeared: derailment.** Uniform random moves along the whole path destroy the +20 far more often than they stumble onto a -10-avoiding detour, and the entropy-regularisation literature independently documents this exact "raise exploration → performance degrades" failure; targeted perturbation at the decision cell plus self-imitation on the rare successful detour is what these papers substitute.

5. **The reward geometry is stacked against us and two of our choices made it worse.** A +2 detour margin against a ~50-point solve/cap spread is a ~4% signal, Dr.GRPO's removal of std-normalisation strips the very amplifier that would rescue low-variance groups, and the per-step/per-mine costs are summed into one episode return before any credit is assigned — so potential-based shaping (Ng et al.), a larger mine price, per-decision advantage (arXiv:2505.11821), and a count-based bonus over off-path cells are all cheaper levers than anything on the algorithm side.
